Why I travel.

I travel because it keeps showing me what's possible — in work, in people, in me.

Two months in Mexico City, living next to a friend from Tennessee who'd been there a year already, reframed what a life could look like. The default 9-to-5 isn't the only way. I knew that in theory. Living it for sixty days rewrote it into instinct.

Going back to Bangladesh at 29 — ten years after my last visit — rewrote what "home" means to me. I went the first time right out of high school. I went back with six years of work behind me, my parents beside me, and a mother whose face I'll never forget in that airport.

In Saudi Arabia for Umrah, I watched a million strangers move toward one thing together. I'm not particularly religious. I felt it anyway.

And in Tokyo, walking through a city where a 7-Eleven operates better than most US restaurants, I learned what "great" can feel like at an operational level I hadn't considered before.

Every trip is an input to the work. Empathy for users. Standards for craft. A steadier sense of what's possible.

— Hussain
